01

Positions

How I see the industry. These are the views I work from. Each one comes with how confident I am and the evidence that would make me drop it. A view you cannot lose is not worth much.

  1. P1

    Attribution is a probability distribution.

    A crypto customer sees an ad on her phone, signs up a week later on a laptop, clears KYC three days after that and funds from her banking app. No click path survives that journey, and since the mobile privacy changes most of it is invisible anyway. I treat channel credit as a range: MMP data, holdout tests and media-mix models triangulated, with the uncertainty reported next to the number.

    What would change my mind
    A privacy-safe, cross-device identifier becomes standard on both major mobile platforms.
    Confidence85%
  2. P2

    In emerging markets, distribution beats acquisition.

    Clicks are cheap in Lagos, Nairobi and Lusaka. Funded, retained accounts are not. Whoever already owns the customer relationship and the payment rail (mobile money operators, banks, merchant networks) carries trust that paid media cannot buy. The best growth plan in these markets often looks more like a partnership strategy than a media plan.

    What would change my mind
    Paid-acquired cohorts in African markets match partner-sourced cohorts on twelve-month retention and payback, at scale.
    Confidence75%
  3. P3

    Crypto demand follows liquidity, so read campaigns against the market.

    Signups, deposits and volume move with the market far more than with any campaign. Bitcoin still trades as a macro asset alongside real yields, the dollar and ETF flows. I plan spend against that backdrop: lean in as liquidity improves, protect retention when it tightens, and never read a bull-market CPA as a channel win.

    What would change my mind
    Bitcoin's relationship with real yields and global liquidity breaks down for a full cycle.
    Confidence70%
  4. P4

    Bad data fails before bad marketing does.

    Most of the "this channel stopped working" debates I have investigated ended in the event pipeline: a redirect that fires a second page view, an SDK that double-counts installs, a consent setting that quietly drops a market. Audit the instrumentation before you judge the channel. It is slower and less fun, and it is usually where the answer is.

    What would change my mind
    I audit a mature marketing data stack and find it clean. It has not happened yet.
    Confidence90%
  5. P5

    Scenarios beat forecasts.

    A single forecast in crypto gets anchored on, then embarrassed. I work in conditional branches instead: two to four scenarios, each with its assumptions written down and a set of observable tells that show which branch we are actually in. When a tell fires, the plan changes. More on how this works.

    What would change my mind
    Point forecasts consistently lead to better decisions than scenario planning on the same questions.
    Confidence80%
  6. P6

    Trust is the product, and it is always under attack.

    Exchange brands get cloned, impersonated in ads and buried under spam SEO. Users cannot tell the difference, so every scam spends the real brand's trust. Competitive intelligence in crypto has to watch bad actors as closely as competitors, and the response is part marketing, part security work.

    What would change my mind
    Ad platforms and search engines bring brand impersonation under durable control.
    Confidence70%

Selected work

Personal project · Python

VisibleMarkets

A market insights generator that pulls from FRED, yfinance, Farside ETF flows, DeFiLlama and exchange APIs. It produces a stance, an attention signal (QUIET, WATCH or BREAK) and the falsifiable tells behind each call. It runs on a written epistemics framework: scenarios over forecasts, assumptions stated, every claim checkable.

Analytics forensics

The phantom page views

A newsroom article was reporting far more views than it could plausibly have had. Live, instrumented browser traces showed a CDN geo-detection redirect to a localised URL that fired a second page view inside the same session. The article was fine. The redirect was the problem.

Marketing operations

Joined as a data analyst and found paid media spending with no clear owner. Took it on and rebuilt it around measurement, so spend could be judged on funded accounts rather than clicks. That is how an analyst ended up running marketing operations.
